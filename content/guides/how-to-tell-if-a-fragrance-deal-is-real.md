title: How to tell if a fragrance deal is actually a good deal
description: Five checks to separate genuine fragrance discounts from inflated "was" prices, before you buy.

A big red "50% off" label doesn't always mean a good price. Here are five quick checks to tell a real deal from a marketing one.

## 1. Ignore the "was" price

Retailers often compare against the recommended retail price (RRP) or a short-lived higher price. Few people ever paid it. What matters is what the bottle has **actually** sold for recently.

That's why The Ledger compares each price against the item's own 30-day median at that retailer, not against its RRP.

## 2. Compare the same size and concentration

A 100ml eau de toilette and a 100ml eau de parfum are different products at different prices. So are 50ml and 100ml bottles. Make sure you're comparing like for like before deciding a price is low.

**Price per ml** is the fairest comparison. Divide the price by the bottle size.

## 3. Check more than one retailer

The same bottle can sell for very different prices at different UK shops on the same day. A "sale" at one shop can still be more expensive than the normal price at another.

Each product page on The Ledger lists today's price at every retailer we track.

## 4. Look at the price history

One day's price tells you very little. A chart of the past month tells you whether today is unusually low or just ordinary. If you see the same "deal" price most weeks, it's not really a deal.

## 5. Be wary of prices that look too good

If a bottle is far cheaper than every major retailer, ask why. Common reasons include testers (often sold without a box or cap), very old stock, or counterfeits. See our guide on [avoiding fake fragrances](avoid-fake-fragrances-online.html).

## A simple rule

A good deal is **in stock, at an authorised retailer, and clearly below what that bottle normally sells for**. If all three are true, buy with confidence.
